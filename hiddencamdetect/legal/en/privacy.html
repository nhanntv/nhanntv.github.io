Privacy Policy

Last updated: August 2026

HiddenCamDetect (the "App") is a hidden-device awareness and triage tool that helps you look for hidden cameras, covert recorders, and eavesdropping devices. This Privacy Policy explains what data the App does and does not handle, and how your privacy is protected. HiddenCamDetect is provided by HiddenCamDetect ("we", "us", or "our").

HiddenCamDetect is offline-first and privacy-first. Your scan results are processed entirely on your device. The App has no scan backend, and your scan data is never uploaded to us or to anyone else.

Data We Do Not Collect

We do not collect, transmit, or store the following on any server. All of it stays on your device and never leaves it:

  • Wi-Fi and network scan results — IP addresses, device names, ports, and services discovered on your local network.
  • Bluetooth (BLE) scan results — nearby broadcasting devices, signal strength, and identifiers.
  • Camera frames — images captured during lens-glint or infrared checks are analyzed on-device in real time and are never saved or sent.
  • Sensor readings — magnetometer and other motion-sensor values are shown live and are not retained off-device.
  • Scan history and baselines — stored only in the App's local database on your device.

Because this data never leaves your device, we cannot see it, access it, or share it.

Permissions We Use and Why

The App requests device permissions only in context and for a specific detection purpose. You can grant or deny each one, and revoke them later in your device settings.

  • Camera — to detect lens reflections (glint) and infrared light from hidden cameras. Frames are analyzed on-device and never stored or transmitted.
  • Location / nearby Wi-Fi (Android) — required by the operating system to scan for networked devices on your local network. We do not track your geographic location.
  • Local Network (iOS) — to discover devices on your local network for scanning.
  • Bluetooth — to scan for nearby broadcasting devices. On Android this is flagged as "never for location", so it is not used to derive your location.
  • Motion sensors — to read the magnetic field as a reference indicator. This signal is informational only.

Diagnostics and Analytics

The App uses two separate Firebase services. They have different switches and different defaults, so each one is described on its own below.

  • Crash reports and basic app data — on by default. Firebase Crashlytics sends a report when the App crashes. Alongside it, the Firebase Analytics SDK collects a small set of standard measurements on its own: app opens, session starts and app updates, your app version, device model and language, an approximate location derived from your IP address, and an app-installation identifier created by Firebase. This collection starts when the App launches, which means it also happens before you reach the consent screen.
  • App usage analytics — off by default. Only if you switch this on, the App additionally sends which screens you open and a short, fixed list of actions: starting a scan, finishing or cancelling a scan (together with the resulting risk level, the number of suspicious devices found, and how long the scan took), opening a detection tool, viewing the upgrade screen, starting, completing or restoring a purchase, finishing the initial introduction, and exporting a PDF report.

Two switches, two different defaults. Crash reports and basic app data are on by default; you can turn them off at any time in Settings → "Share anonymous diagnostics". App usage analytics are off by default and stay off until you switch them on yourself — either on the consent screen shown the first time you open the App, or later in Settings → "Share app usage analytics". Turning either switch off does not disable any detection feature. Neither service is used for cross-app tracking or advertising (no IDFA).

We call the first switch "anonymous" because the data carries no name, email address, account or advertising identifier, and we never try to work out who you are from it. Even so, we would rather be precise than reassuring: Firebase assigns an identifier to your installation of the App, and it always sees your IP address and the time of each event. If you switch app usage analytics on, the risk level and the number of suspicious devices found in a scan are sent alongside that identifier. That is a real trade-off, and you should know about it before you decide.

Your scan data is never included in diagnostics or in usage analytics, and is never sent anywhere. No network name (SSID), BSSID or MAC address, IP address, hostname, device name, room or network label, file path, or detection detail ever leaves your device through either service. The App enforces this in its own code: the usage analytics layer accepts only numbers, true/false flags and choices from a closed list — never free text — screen names come from a fixed list of static screens that never contain an identifier, and any value that still resembles an address or identifier causes the whole event to be discarded. The only scan-related values that can ever leave your device are the overall risk level and the count of suspicious devices, and only while app usage analytics are switched on. This is a core commitment that does not change.

Detection Data Updates

The App may download public reference data over a secure HTTPS connection — for example, hardware vendor (OUI) lists and known suspicious port lists — to improve detection accuracy. This is a download only. No scan data or personal information is sent as part of these updates.

In-App Purchases

The App offers an optional paid subscription ("Pro"). Payments are processed entirely by the Apple App Store or Google Play. We do not receive or store your payment card details. Purchase and subscription status is validated through the store's receipt mechanism. Please refer to the applicable store's privacy policy for how your payment information is handled.

We use Qonversion to manage and validate subscriptions. Qonversion receives your purchase and subscription status and an anonymous, app-generated identifier; it does not receive your payment card details. See Qonversion's privacy policy at https://qonversion.io/privacy.

Local Storage

The App stores your scan history, baselines, and settings (such as theme, language, and enabled modules) locally on your device using an on-device database and preferences. This information is not synced to any server.

Children's Privacy

The App is not directed to children under the age of 13 (or the minimum age required in your jurisdiction, such as 16 in parts of the EU). We do not knowingly collect personal information from children.

Your Rights

Depending on where you live, you may have rights under laws such as the GDPR or CCPA, including the right to access or delete your personal data. Because the App does not collect or store your scan data or personal data on any server, all of your data lives on your device. Deleting the App removes this data, including your scan history and settings. You can turn crash reports and basic app data off, and turn app usage analytics on or off, at any time in Settings.

For any privacy question or request, contact us using the details below.

Changes to This Policy

We may update this Privacy Policy from time to time. When we make material changes, we will update the "Last updated" date above and, where appropriate, notify you within the App. Your continued use of the App after changes take effect means you accept the updated policy.
